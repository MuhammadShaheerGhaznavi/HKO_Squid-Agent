## AIRAC and NOTAM Procedures

- Operationally significant changes are published as AIP AMDT or AIP SUP on predetermined AIRAC dates.
- AIRAC information must be available at least 28 days in advance; major changes require at least 56 days.
- A Trigger NOTAM is issued after publication and remains in force for 14 days after the AIRAC date.
- NOTAMs are distributed in Series A (international) and Series C (local), with serial numbers consecutive per calendar year.
- Foreign NOTAM offices can retrieve Hong Kong NOTAMs by sending RQN messages to VHHHYNYX, e.g., 'RQN VHHH A0115/14' or 'RQN VHHH A0201/14 - A0205/14'.
- SNOWTAM is issued when the runway at HKIA is contaminated by standing water.
- AIRAC dates for 2026: 22 Jan, 19 Feb, 19 Mar, 16 Apr, 14 May, 11 Jun, 9 Jul, 6 Aug, 3 Sep, 1 Oct, 29 Oct, 26 Nov, 24 Dec.

## Altimeter Setting Procedures

- Transition altitude: 9,000 ft; transition level: FL110.
- When QNH at Hong Kong International Airport is 979 hPa or below, ATC informs pilots of the transition level via ATIS or voice. Lowest QNH recorded: 953.2 hPa.
- Aircraft beyond 50 NM from HKIA, or within 50 NM but at or above transition level, use 1013.2 hPa.
- Aircraft within 50 NM at or below transition altitude use local QNH.
- Aircraft wishing to operate within 50 NM shall not flight plan to cruise between transition altitude and FL120.
- Arriving aircraft change from 1013.2 to QNH when 50 NM from airport if at or below 9,000 ft, or when vacating transition level on descent.
- Departing aircraft change from QNH to 1013.2 when 50 NM from airport if at or below 9,000 ft, or when vacating transition altitude on climb.
- QNH reports available on ATIS (128.2 MHz and 127.05 MHz); QFE on request. All values in hectopascals, rounded down.
- Vertical separation minima: 1,000 ft at or below FL410, 2,000 ft above FL410.
- Minimum rate of change of 500 feet per minute is assumed; pilots intending lower rate must inform ATC.
- VFR cruising levels: magnetic track 000°-179° odd thousands plus 500 ft; 180°-359° even thousands plus 500 ft.

## General Rules and Communication Procedures

- Air traffic rules in Hong Kong FIR conform to Annex 2 and Annex 11, the Air Navigation (Hong Kong) Order 1995, ICAO Doc 4444 PANS/ATM, and Regional Supplementary Procedures MID/ASIA Region, except for differences in GEN 1.7.
- For traffic entering Hong Kong FIR, aircraft must establish two-way radio communication with Hong Kong Radar on specified frequencies at least 10 NM before certain reporting points (or 3 minutes for others).
- Reporting points and frequencies (PRI primary, SRY secondary):
  - A1(E)/G581 at ELATO: 121.3 (PRI), 128.125 (SRY)
  - A202/R339 at SIKOU: 127.1 (PRI), 123.7 (SRY)
  - A461/M501 at NOMAN: 132.15 (PRI), 129.9 (SRY)
  - A470 at DOTMI: 121.3 (PRI), 128.125 (SRY)
  - A583 at SABNO: 128.75 (PRI), 129.9 (SRY)
  - B330 at TAMOT: 127.1 (PRI), 123.7 (SRY)
  - G86 at KAPLI: 132.15 (PRI), 129.9 (SRY)
  - M503 at LELIM: 121.3 (PRI), 128.125 (SRY)
  - M771 at DOSUT: 122.95 (PRI), 135.6 (SRY) and at DUMOL: 128.75 (PRI), 129.9 (SRY)
  - M772 at ASOBA: 122.95 (PRI), 135.6 (SRY)
  - A1(W) at IKELA: 125.325 (PRI), 132.775 (SRY)
  - R473 at SIERA: 127.55 (PRI), 134.3 (SRY)
  - ATS MCU DVOR/DME: 123.95 (PRI), 132.225 (SRY)
  - ATS ROMEO: 123.95 (PRI), 132.225 (SRY)
- Initial call must include call sign, position (relative to reporting point), level (including passing and cleared levels), transponder code, and other pertinent information.
- Aircraft entering outside controlled airspace but wishing to join must request clearance and remain clear until received.
- After take-off, on first contact with 'Hong Kong Departure', pilot states call sign, passing altitude to nearest 100 ft, and assigned altitude.
- Approach Control uses phraseology 'contact Hong Kong Director 119.5 MHz with call sign only' to reduce congestion.
- Aircraft leaving Hong Kong FIR remain on Hong Kong control frequency until instructed.
- Position reports are made over or as soon as possible after passing reporting points; radar-identified aircraft may omit reports when informed, but must resume when instructed or when radar service terminates.
- Pilots must read back: ATC route clearances, all clearances to enter, land on, take off on, cross and backtrack runway, other clearances including conditional clearances, runway in use, altimeter settings, SSR codes, level instructions, heading and speed instructions, and transition levels when required.

## Content
When intercepted, the pilot must immediately: follow instructions from intercepting aircraft, interpret and respond to visual signals, notify ATS unit if possible, attempt radio communication on 121.5 MHz (and 243 MHz if no contact), select Mode A Code 7700 on SSR transponder, and select emergency functionality on ADS-B/ADS-C if available. If radio instructions conflict with visual signals, request clarification while complying with visual instructions. If radio instructions conflict with each other, request clarification while complying with radio instructions. Radio communication phrases for intercepting aircraft include: CALL SIGN (KOL-SA-IN), FOLLOW (FOL-LO), DESCEND (DEE-SEND), YOU LAND (YOU LAAND), PROCEED (PRO-SEED). For intercepted aircraft: CALL SIGN (KOL-SA-IN), WILCO (VILL-KO), CAN NOT (KANN NOTT), REPEAT (REE-PEET), AM LOST (AM LOSST), MAYDAY (MAYDAY), HIJACK (HI-JACK), LAND (LAAND), DESCEND (DEE-SEND). Visual signals: Series 1 - intercepting aircraft rocks wings and flashes lights, meaning 'You have been intercepted. Follow me.'; intercepted aircraft responds by rocking wings and flashing lights. Series 2 - abrupt breakaway, meaning 'You may proceed.'; response is rocking aircraft. Series 3 - lowering landing gear and overflying runway, meaning 'Land at this aerodrome.'; response is lowering gear and following. Series 4 - intercepted aircraft raises gear and flashes lights, meaning 'Aerodrome you have designated is inadequate.'; intercepting aircraft responds with Series 1 or 2 signals. Series 5 - regular switching on/off of lights, meaning 'Cannot comply.'; response is Series 2. Series 6 - irregular flashing, meaning 'In distress.'; response is Series 2. See [[Unlawful Interference]].
